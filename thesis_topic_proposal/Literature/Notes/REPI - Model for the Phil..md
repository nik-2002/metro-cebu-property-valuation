## 1. Introduction
- The **[[Real Estate Price Index (REPI)]]** is a valuable tool for assessing and valuing real properties.
>Most countries don't have an established system of real estate prices (Domingo and Fulleros,  2005)
- The Philippines needs an REPI due to **shortcomings** in the **existing zonal valuation system**, which leads to **undervaluation of properties** affecting government revenue, financial stability, and land management.
- The **National Statistical Coordination Board (NSCB)** is working on developing an appropriate REPI system.
- This system aims to provide consistent measures of price developments over time and cater to the needs of various stakeholders, including government, banks, and real estate developers.

## 2. Conditions of Real Property Valuation in the Philippines
- The Philippine property market faces **inefficiency** and **inequity**, stifling economic development and discouraging sustainable resource management.
- Main issues include:
  - **Outdated zonal valuation** system.
  - **Inconsistent pricing** due to multiple valuation systems across different government agencies.
  - **Lack of trained appraisers** and poor understanding of property valuation by local officials.
  - A **lack of data** on property movements.
- The current property market requires a **comprehensive property administration database** and a reliable REPI system.

## 3. Conceptual Framework

### 3.1 Scope and Coverage
- Real estate in the Philippines includes:
  1. **Land and buildings** or improvements.
  2. **Rural, suburban, and urban land** areas, including residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, and other types.
  3. **Special developments** such as resorts, reclamation areas, and housing projects.
  
- The study focuses on **agricultural, residential, and commercial properties**.

### 3.2 Present Classification/Structure of Market
- Real estate transactions include:
  1. Sales
  2. Rental/lease
  3. Donations
  4. Mortgages
- The market is classified into several categories:
  1. **Agricultural** (crops, livestock, etc.)
  2. **Residential** (regular and condominium)
  3. **Commercial** (regular and condominium)
  4. **Industrial** (factories, warehouses)
  5. **General purpose** (raw land for development)

### 3.3 Proposed Composition of the Real Estate Price Index
- The REPI structure will cover various categories of real estate:
  - **Agricultural**: By crop type, location, and cost.
  - **Forest**: By forest type and location.
  - **Residential**: By type (regular, condominium), cost, size, and social category.
  - **Commercial**: By type (regular, condominium), size, and cost.
  - **Industrial**: By type (factory, warehouse), size, and location.

## 4. Assessment of Data

### 4.1 National Statistics Office (NSO)
- NSO provides data from **Census of Population and Housing (CPH)** and **Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES)**, which are key for property characteristics and price indices.

### 4.2 Land Registration Administration (LRA)
- LRA maintains records of property titles and transfers, crucial for REPI data.

### 4.3 Records of Provincial/City/Municipality Assessors
- Local assessors keep records of property declarations for taxation, providing useful data for the REPI.

### 4.4 Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR)
- BIR determines property values for taxation purposes, which can help in the REPI but has limitations like **undervaluation** and lack of professional standards.

### 4.5 Other Data Sources
- Other sources include the **Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board (HLURB)**, **DENR**, and **PEZA** for additional land-use and property information.

### 4.6 Data Problems
- Existing data is often unprocessed, requiring **cleaning** and **organization** to be useful for the REPI.
- Surveys also need improvements for better coverage.

## 5. Proposed Real Estate Price Index Methodology

### 5.1 General Strategy
- Start with **pilot cities** for testing, then replicate elsewhere.
- Use a combination of **survey and administrative data** for compilation.
- Ensure **validation** from public and private stakeholders.
- **NSCB** will lead compilation to avoid bias and ensure sustainability.

### 5.2 Data Sources
- Data from the **2000 CPH**, **BIR records**, **LRA** property sales, **CPI**, and **PEZA** will be used.

### 5.3 Methodology

#### 5.3.1 Assumptions
- The **property mix** remains constant over a given period (e.g., 5 years).
- **Actual sales data** from LRA/BIR is representative.
- Local assessor records are adequate for REPI purposes.

#### 5.3.2 Estimation
- **Stock of properties**: Data will be collected from NSO, BIR, LRA, and other sources.
- **Prices**: Two indices will be built—Sales Price Index and Rental Price Index.
  - Prices will be derived from **actual transactions** or **hedonic pricing methods** if no direct data is available.
- **Index Calculation**: Use **Laspeyres Index** with 2000 as the base year.

## 6. Future Directions
- **Preparatory Activities**:
  - Identify **pilot cities** for REPI estimation.
  - Set up **inter-agency committee** for REPI development.
  - Advocate for REPI usage in taxation and policy formulation.
- **Data Preparation and Improvement**:
  - Improve data collection and processing for REPI.
- **Estimation**:
  - Develop property basket and refine REPI methodology.
- **Computerisation**:
  - Automate REPI compilation and dissemination.
- **Documentation**:
  - Document the REPI model and create an institutionalisation plan.
- **Capability Building**:
  - Provide training for those involved in property valuation.
