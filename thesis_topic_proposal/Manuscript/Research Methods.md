## Literature Review
also know as a review of related lit.

### Identified the Gap of the Research
Gap between the **Expectation** and **Reality**
What you thought would happen - what actually happened

### Purpose of a Lit. Review
#### Define
- proved the meaning/definitions for each key concept in your study, as used in the previous researches
#### Characterize
- what consists this key concept?
- define attributes
#### Relate
- discusses the relationships of the concepts in your study, as discovers in previous researches.
#### Anticipate
- discusses the implications/relevance of the relationships discovered in the previous studies.
### Purpose of RRL and the Level of Analysis

|               |              |
| ------------- | ------------ |
| Surface Level | Define       |
| ^             | Characterize |
| Deep-Dive     | Relate       |
| ^             | Anticipate   |
## How to Start Lit. Review
1. Start with identifying your **key words**.
2. Go to **library or online databases** and begin searching about your key words.
3. Prioritize **books and journals** in your searching.
	1. *after* checking for books and journals, then you can try using blogs and articles.
4. Try to locate **15 sources** related to your research on your topic.
5. Skim through this initial group of articles or chapters
6. Decide which sources will be useful for your topic
7. Once you decided, prepare a literature catalog where you categorize the sources collected into the 4 purposes of the RRL

| **Define**                                  | **Characterize**                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| - Def of Related lit.                       | - attributes of Good writing: Calabia, C. Tips on writing             |
| **Relate**                                  | **Anticipate**                                                        |
| Impact of Good Writing on Client Management | - Why Client Management is Important: Lee, D. Client Management: p.74 |

>you need to know which source a particular concept is attributed to...

8. Create a **literature map**, illustrating how the concepts/themes connects with each other.
	- this will be your **conceptual framework**
9. Draft **one-page summaries** for each of the relevant sources you collected.
10. Outline your **literature review draft**.


## Drafting your Literature Review: Outline

|               |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Surface Level | **Define**<br>• One section to discuss the *definition* of key words/concepts<br>• Feel free to add subsections per key concept                                                                                                            |

## Research Design
**Types:**
1. Quantitative
2. Qualitative
3. Mixed Methods
### Qualitative Research
is a means for *exploring and understanding the meaning*
### Quantitative Research
is a means for testing objective theories by examining the *relationship* among variables.
### Mixed Methods
is an approach to inquiry that combines or associates both qualitative and quantitative.

## Research Population
it discusses **who you are going to study**. Usually, is is the focal stakeholder.
### What needs to be discussed
- the source of data points
	- specify where you will get the data
	- if it is an organization, provide a background about it.
	- if is a database, discuss what this database is about.
- the sample size of data points
	- specify how many data points will you get.
	- if is a time series data, discuss the timeframe covered.
	- explain why you are using that particular sample size and timeframe.
## Data Instrument
- the instrument
	-  briefly discuss what teh instrument is about.
	- 
- the reliability of the instrument
- variable - question - objective matrix

## Data Gathering
it discusses how you will select your who - that is, your data points/respondents
### What needs to be discussed
- sampling method
	- discuss how the data will be filtered
- strategies for data collection
	- narrate how the data collection will proceed. be as specific and logical as possiblie in your narration
	- if you can visualize the process in a chart.
	- if you can include specific dates for dissemination, it will be ideal.
	- explain very clearly why you're taking such a strategy.
## Data Treatment
- The model
	- introduce the method/model of analysis you will use
	- explain why the model is fitting for the study - refer to your objectives
	- outline the metrics/diagnostics you willl to determine the model's validity.
	- objectively discuss the strengths and imitations of your model
- model formulation
	- if using an equation, present the equation connecting the dependent variable with the independent variables.
	- state the hypotheses you want to test.
	- outline the hypothesized impact of each independent variable on the dependent variable, as described in your RRL.
	- if no equation, just outline the variables in a matrix, including the sources where they will be taken.
- empirical framework
	- illustrate in a chart similar to the literature map the interconnection of the variables outlined in Model Formialation.
	- Provide a short write-up to narrate the framework
