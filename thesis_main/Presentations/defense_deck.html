The Question

20
The business problem

No defensible, market-facing price reference for Metro Cebu homes.

21
The data problem

Turn a property — and where it sits — into a defensible price per square meter.

  • Property-level data at scale, drawn from online listings.
  • Location turned into measurable features — distances, accessibility, neighborhood price.
  • A model that generalizes honestly, and explains its reasoning.
22
Objectives
  • Predictive — estimate open-market price per square meter across Metro Cebu.
  • Prescriptive — show where those estimates diverge from official benchmarks.
23
Research Questions
  • 1   What value drivers influence price?
  • 2   Which model deploys best?
  • 3   Do geospatial features help?
  • 4   How large is the BIR gap?
24
The solution

A stratified, explainable ML model — delivered as a prototype for triangulation.

25
Roadmap — CRISP-DM
Business→Data→Prep→Modeling→Evaluation→Deployment

The Field

27
Two traditions
  • Hedonic regression — interpretable, classic.
  • Machine learning — flexible, higher accuracy.
Rosen, 1974; Breiman, 2001
28
What drives price in the literature
  • Structural
  • Economic / benchmark
  • Geospatial / accessibility
  • Amenity / point-of-interest
29
International evidence
  • Tree models tend to outperform linear models on tabular property data.
  • But small samples demand caution.
Grinsztajn et al., 2022; Tanamal et al., 2023
30
Philippine &amp; Cebu evidence
  • ML work is mostly Manila / Pangasinan-centric.
  • Agosto (2020) is the only Cebu study — transport accessibility is the primary driver.
Viray, 2023; Ramolete et al., 2023; Agosto, 2020
31
The gap

Nothing property-level, geospatial, and explainable — for Metro Cebu.

32
Our bridge

This study builds exactly that.

  • Property-level — every listing geocoded across the six LGUs.
  • Open-market — aligned to the IVS Market Value basis.
  • Geospatial and explainable — with SHAP behind every prediction.
33
Why open-market listings?

The closest available proxy to IVS Market Value.

Arm's-length asking evidence — the basis a willing buyer and seller actually meet on.
IVSC, 2025

Why geospatial features?

Location is price — so we construct the location signal, not ingest it.

41
MCRAI — what it is

Metro Cebu Residential Accessibility Index (MCRAI)

MCRAIic = Σj∈c 1 / max(dij, 0.5)β    (β = 2)
Nearer amenities count more — accessibility decays with the square of road-distance.
Hansen, 1959
42
MCRAI — how it's built
Eight amenity categories, each with its own radius. The composite keeps only positive-signal categories.
43
MCRAI — how one property scores
  • Each category has its own catchment radius (1–5 km).
  • Nearer amenities count more — inverse-square decay (β = 2).
  • Transport enters separately, as road-network distance.
Hansen, 1959
44
Engineered location — spatial lag
Mean price of nearby same-type listings within 500 m.
Tobler, 1970
45
Price per square meter is skewed
  • Price per square meter is right-skewed — a few premium units stretch the tail.
  • Modeled in log: it tames the skew but keeps a per-unit meaning.
  • Predictions are back-transformed for the price surface.
46
Price varies across the map
  • Premium signal sits in the core and island: Cebu City ~₱113,600/sqm, Mandaue ~₱96,100, Lapu-Lapu ~₱92,100.
  • Talisay, Minglanilla, Consolacion form a lower band (~₱47–57k).
  • Location can't be assumed uniform — it has to be modeled.
47
Do we trust the features?
  • Several CBD-distance features move together — the southern corridor is geographically nested.
  • A flag for the linear baseline, not the trees: OLS trimmed the collinear terms (confirmed with VIF).
  • Random Forest tolerates correlated predictors, so the meaningful nodes stayed.

06

Groundwork

49
The finding that changed everything
  • The condo median runs ≈ 5.8× the vacant-lot median.
  • Built area vs land-only are different price logics.
  • A single pooled model would blur them — so we stratify by type.
50
Cleaning, honestly

Cleaning kept every decision visible.

  • Imputed values were flagged, never silently filled.
  • Structurally-absent fields (beds/baths for lots) left blank, not faked.
  • Hard duplicates dropped; a price-per-sqm sanity band applied.
51
A data-integrity story

One feature was quietly hijacking the target.

  • A scale-selector feature dominated the SHAP rankings.
  • The target had come to mean two different things across scrape batches.
  • Tracing and fixing it is why the final metrics can be trusted.

The model lineup
  • OLS — interpretable hedonic baseline.
  • Random Forest & XGBoost — capture non-linear, interacting effects.
  • SHAP — explains every prediction, feature by feature.
Rosen, 1974; Breiman, 2001; Chen &amp; Guestrin, 2016; Lundberg &amp; Lee, 2017
57
Honest evaluation

GroupKFold by location — the honest test.

  • Listings are grouped by coordinate cluster.
  • The same spot never appears in both train and test.
  • Stricter than a random 80/20 split — it blocks neighborhood memorization.
58
Random Forest deployed (RQ2)
  • Tree models beat OLS in every stratum.
  • RF edged XGBoost (19.3 vs 19.8 / 22.7 vs 23.6 / 38.4 vs 40.2 MdAPE).
  • Deployed for robustness on small samples and simplicity.

61
What that means
  • MdAPE — half of estimates fall within this error.
  • PE20 — the share landing within 20% (the practical hit-rate).
  • Reported with MAPE, COD, PRD — but not claiming IAAO compliance.
62
Do geospatial features help? (RQ3)
  • Geospatial features improve every stratum over structural-only.
  • Biggest lift where benchmarks are weakest: vacant lots (+13 pts) and condos (+5.7).
  • For houses, city + BIR zonal already capture most of the location signal.
63
What drives price — condominiums (RQ1)
  • Neighborhood price level (spatial lag) leads — condos track their block.
  • Weight spreads across several nodes — Consolacion, airport, Mactan, CBP.
  • The signature of a multi-node, polycentric market.
64
What drives price — houses (RQ1)
  • Distance to Cebu Business Park is the single strongest driver.
  • Classic bid-rent: detached-house value falls with distance from the core.
  • Amenities enter only as the bundled MCRAI composite.
65
What drives price — vacant lots (RQ1)
  • Distance to Cebu Business Park leads — the steepest bid-rent gradient of the three.
  • Individual amenities are the second-largest driver — bare land responds to specific nearby access.
  • Still the weakest stratum: unrecorded parcel attributes (frontage, zoning) cap accuracy.
66
The valuation gap (RQ4)
  • Market prices run ~3× the BIR zonal benchmark for vacant lots.
  • Systematic, not noise — listings beat BIR in nearly every LGU.
  • A signal that benchmarks are stale — not a correction factor to apply.
67
Is it good enough?
  • A triangulation reference, not a replacement.
  • Strongest for condos and houses; indicative for lots.
  • It complements professional judgment — never replaces it.

The Close

73
Answers to the four questions
  • RQ1 — Location dominates; drivers differ by type.
  • RQ2 — Random Forest deployed.
  • RQ3 — Geospatial helps, most where benchmarks are weak.
  • RQ4 — The BIR gap is large and systematic.
74
Contribution
  • Methodological — per-type modeling, polycentric distances, the two-stage MCRAI, leak-free CV.
  • Practical — a reproducible, explainable web prototype.
75
Limitations
  • Asking-price ceiling (not deed-of-sale).
  • Cross-sectional snapshot.
  • Vacant-lot data ceiling.
76
Recommendations — practice &amp; policy
  • Use as triangulation, not a final number.
  • Recognize secondary subcenters; MCRAI as a template, not a finished index.
77
Recommendations — future research
  • Estimate MCRAI parameters from data.
  • Enrich parcel attributes; add a time dimension.
Udomsap &amp; Abid, 2020
