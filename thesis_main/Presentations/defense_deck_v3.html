Introduction

The valuation problem in Metro Cebu
3 / 39
1 · Introduction3 / 39
Where this study is set
  • Metro Cebu is the country's second-largest urban region; this study focuses on its central core of six LGUs — Cebu City, Mandaue, Lapu-Lapu, Talisay, Minglanilla, Consolacion.
  • A fast-growing economy: 7.3% growth in 2024, among the fastest outside Metro Manila (PSA Cebu, 2025).
  • And a fast-rising housing market: 11.5% residential price growth in 2025, the highest rate outside the NCR (BSP, 2025).
  • Major infrastructure (CBRT, Cebu–Cordova Expressway, SRP) is redrawing which barangays count as accessible.
TakeawayPrices are moving faster than the references people use to interpret them.

1 · Introduction4 / 39
How a price is decided — four partial references

A family buying, or an owner selling, asks the same question: what is this property worth? There is no single public source — price is pieced together from several partial references, each built to answer a different question.

ReferenceBuilt forWhat it misses
Broker & agent opinionLocal market knowledgeNot standardized or reproducible across people
Bank appraisalsLending & collateral riskConservative; not the seller's market question
BIR zonal valuesTaxation & assessmentNot live prices; often not updated (BIR, n.d.; Otsuka et al., 2023)
Online listingsCurrent, property-levelAsking prices — full of seller strategy and noise
They serve different purposes, follow different assumptions, and update at different speeds — so they cannot be compared.

The problem
Metro Cebu lacks a transparent, property-level, spatially detailed reference for interpreting residential price evidence.
TakeawayThis study builds that missing reference layer — a decision-support tool, not a replacement for professional appraisal (IVSC, 2025).

Review of Related Literature

What is known, and the gap
8 / 39
2 · Review of Related Literature8 / 39
The obstacle, and two modeling traditions

International work consistently finds the primary obstacle to valuation in developing markets is data scarcity — not valuer misconduct.

The evidence
  • Kenya — a census of 427 Nairobi valuers ranked “limited information” the top problem (Cheloti, 2021).
  • Nigeria — 92.7% of 300 valuers cited insufficient market evidence (Ajibola, 2010).
  • The fix is not better judgment but a better, shared evidence base.
Two modeling traditions
  • Hedonic regression — interpretable, one coefficient per attribute (Rosen, 1974).
  • Machine learning — non-linear, interacting effects (Breiman, 2001); tree ensembles win on tabular data (Grinsztajn et al., 2022).
  • Regional precedent in Surabaya (Tanamal et al., 2023), with small-sample caution.
9 / 39
2 · Review of Related Literature9 / 39
Location dominates — and why we model types separately
  • Location is the dominant driver. Agosto (2020), the only Cebu-specific study, finds transport accessibility the primary driver of land value; accessibility is formalized through gravity indexing (Hansen, 1959), since raw distances alone are too fragmented (Rey-Blanco, 2024).
  • Separate models per property type are grounded in evidence: modeling submarkets separately lifted R² from 0.637 to 0.782 (Dröes et al., 2019); segmenting before fitting improved fit ~7% and cut error >10% (Usman et al., 2020).
  • Different property types are priced by different logic, so one pooled equation fits all of them worse.
TakeawayThe gap: no published, reproducible, property-level model for Metro Cebu integrates open-market listings, geospatial accessibility, and explainable ML. This study builds it.

12 / 39
3 · Methodology12 / 39
Geospatial features — distance, neighborhood, and the target
  • Shortest-path road-network distance (not straight-line) to eight economic nodes — Cebu Business Park, Mandaue, Mactan, SRP, Talisay Tabunok, Consolacion, Naga, and the airport — grounded in polycentric urban economics (Giuliano & Small, 1991; JICA, 2015).
  • A neighborhood price lag: the average price of nearby same-type listings within 500 m (Tobler, 1970).
  • The model predicts the log of price per square meter; predictions are converted back for the price surface.
  • Road distance captures real travel cost — including the Mactan bridge crossing.
13 / 39
3 · Methodology13 / 39
MCRAI — measuring access to amenities
The idea, in plain terms
  • For each property, count the nearby amenities in a category (schools, groceries, hospitals, …).
  • Closer amenities count more — the score fades with the square of road distance.
  • Each category has its own reach, because a hospital serves a wider area than a corner store.
MCRAIic = Σj∈c 1 / max(dij, 0.5)2

Distances in km, floored at 0.5 km. (Hansen, 1959)

MCRAI — categories, reach, and how the weights were chosen
CategoryReach (km)
Education2.5
Grocery2.0

Hospitals5.0

Tourism3.0

Each reach is set to the category's catchment, refined for Metro Cebu. Transport is measured separately as road distance to the eight nodes.
Weights from the market, in two stages
  • Stage 1 — fit a hedonic regression; each category's coefficient is its implicit price (sign = premium or penalty; significance = reliable) (Rosen, 1974).
  • Stage 2 — keep the positive, significant categories and normalize their coefficients to sum to one.
  • Result: education 0.447, grocery 0.345, recreation 0.222; the others stay as standalone features, not in the composite.
TakeawayThe regression discovers the weights — we do not impose them.

Collection and exploratory analysis

From collection to a clean ABT
  • 16,561 listings scraped across three portals; 3,616 survived to the analytics base table.
  • Filters: geocoding, the six-LGU and residential constraints, a price-per-sqm sanity band, distressed-listing removal, and de-duplication.

TakeawayA conservative funnel: roughly one in five raw listings is kept.
17 / 39
4 · Data Understanding17 / 39
What the data shows
  • Price per sqm is right-skewed (a few premium units) — which is why it is modeled in log.
  • Sharp geographic spread: Cebu City ~₱113,600/sqm, Mandaue ~₱96,100, Lapu-Lapu ~₱92,100; a lower band ~₱47,100–56,800 for Talisay, Minglanilla, Consolacion.
  • Some southern CBD distances move together — an early collinearity warning for the linear baseline (handled with VIF).
  • MCRAI coverage checked before modeling: education and grocery near-complete, security sparsest — zero rates flag thin catchments, not data failure.

Data Preparation

Cleaning, and preparing data for the models
19 / 39
5 · Data Preparation19 / 39
Cleaning, and a data-integrity finding
  • Imputed values were flagged, never silently filled; structurally-absent fields (beds/baths for vacant lots) were left unimputed, not faked.
  • Hard duplicates were dropped and a price-per-square-meter sanity band applied; the modeling subset is 3,372 of the 3,616 assembled rows.
  • Early models were dominated by one suspicious SHAP feature — the target had quietly come to mean two different things across scrape batches.
TakeawayTracing and fixing that — redefining the target to log price per square meter — is exactly why the final metrics are dependable.

Preparing the data for modeling

  • Every candidate passes four screens: variance inflation (drop duplicates), OLS significance (keep what moves price), leave-one-block ablation, and MCRAI zero rates.

Modeling

Stratified models, selection, tuning
22 / 39
6 · Modeling22 / 39
One model cannot price three markets
5.8×
The condominium median price per square meter runs about 5.8 times the vacant-lot median.
  • Built area and land-only follow fundamentally different price logic; a single pooled model averages them into one blurred equation that fits none well.
  • So the study fits three separate models, one per property type — backed by Dröes et al. (2019) and Usman et al. (2020).
TakeawayStratifying is itself an evidence-based finding, not a convenience.
23 / 39
6 · Modeling23 / 39
The three feature sets
Shared core (all three strata)
  • Eight road-network CBD distances.
  • BIR zonal benchmark + 500 m spatial lag.
  • City indicators (Cebu City = reference).
Per-stratum differences
  • Condos & houses: structural attributes (area, beds, baths + flags) and the single MCRAI composite.
  • Houses add property-type indicators (single-detached, house-and-lot, townhouse).
  • Vacant lots: land area only (no beds/baths) + six individual MCRAI categories.
TakeawayCollinearity shaped the OLS baseline (composite and redundant terms trimmed); the deployed trees tolerate it. The strata differ mainly by what each property type has. Final counts: 21 / 24 / 22.
24 / 39
6 · Modeling24 / 39
Models, selection, and tuning
The lineup
  • OLS hedonic — interpretable baseline, HC3 robust errors (Rosen, 1974).
  • Random Forest — non-linear, robust on small samples (Breiman, 2001).
  • XGBoost — gradient boosting (Chen & Guestrin, 2016); SHAP explains every prediction (Lundberg & Lee, 2017).
Why these, and tuning
  • Set aside: SVR (hard to tune, weak interpretability), LASSO/Ridge (subsumed by OLS), deep nets (need >10,000 samples).
  • Deployed Random Forest tuned per stratum (300 trees; max-features 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.0; min-leaf 1 / 2 / 1).
  • Comparators run at standard settings, so the head-to-head stays fair.

7 · Evaluation27 / 39
Which model performed best? (RQ2)
  • Under identical leak-free folds, both tree models beat the OLS baseline in every stratum.
  • Random Forest and XGBoost are close; RF edges it on grouped-CV (19.3 vs 19.8 / 22.7 vs 23.6 / 38.4 vs 40.2).
  • Because the tree models are so close, the fair reading is “RF best-or-tied.”
  • Random Forest was deployed — robust on small samples, and simpler to maintain.
28 / 39
7 · Evaluation28 / 39
Do geospatial features earn their place? (RQ3)
  • A three-tier ablation under identical folds: Structural → + Administrative → + Geospatial.
  • Geospatial features improve every stratum vs structural-only: condo +5.7, houses +4.2, lot +13.0 points.
  • On top of administrative location (city + BIR zonal): condo +3.7, lot +3.8, houses ≈ −0.7.
  • They add the most where benchmarks are weakest — vertical condos and bare land.

Results and Discussion

Drivers and spatial structure
30 / 39
8 · Results & Discussion30 / 39
What drives value, and the polycentric structure (RQ1)
  • Location dominates in every stratum — the geospatial block carries most of the SHAP attribution.
  • Drivers differ by type: condos track their neighborhood price (spatial lag); houses and lots are driven by distance to Cebu Business Park (classic bid-rent); bare land also responds to individual amenity access.
  • MCRAI is selective — education, grocery, recreation carry positive weight; security, tourism, retail behave as diagnostics, not premiums.
  • The model learned a polycentric, not monocentric, structure: CBP anchors land and housing, but condo weight spreads across the Mactan/airport corridor and the Mandaue–Consolacion nodes (Giuliano & Small, 1991; JICA, 2015).
TakeawayNegative MCRAI signs reflect spatial sorting — these uses cluster where congestion deters some buyers (Tiebout, 1956; Bayer & McMillan, 2012) — not proof of harm.
31 / 39
8 · Results & Discussion31 / 39
The valuation gap (RQ4)
  • On the clean land-to-land comparison, vacant-lot market price runs ~3× the BIR benchmark overall.
  • From ~2.2× in Cebu City to ~4.8× in Mandaue; listings exceed the benchmark in nearly every LGU.
  • The gap is large, positive, and systematic — BIR zonal values sit well below the open market.
  • Read it as a research signal that benchmarks are stale — not a correction factor to apply.

Conclusions

Answers, contributions, limits
34 / 39
9 · Conclusions34 / 39
Answers, contributions, and limits
Answers to the research questions
  • RQ1 — Location dominates; drivers differ by type (spatial lag for condos; CBP distance for houses and lots; amenities for bare land).
  • RQ2 — Tree models beat OLS everywhere; RF best-or-tied with XGBoost, deployed for robustness and simplicity.
  • RQ3 — Geospatial features materially help, most where benchmarks are weakest.
  • RQ4 — The BIR gap is large, positive, and systematic (~3× on the clean lot comparison).
Contributions & limits
  • Per-type modeling with evidence-based feature selection; a polycentric distance set; a two-stage MCRAI; all results under leak-free grouped CV.
  • A reproducible, explainable web prototype over the deployed models.
  • Limits: an asking-price ceiling (listings, not deeds); a cross-sectional snapshot; a vacant-lot data ceiling; thin LGU×type cells.

Recommendations

Practice, policy, future work
36 / 39
10 · Recommendations36 / 39
Recommendations
Practice & policy
  • Read the surface as a triangulation tool, alongside zonal values and comparables — not a final appraisal.
  • Weight polycentric distance and property type ahead of the amenity composite.
  • Keep recognizing secondary and corridor subcenters (Mactan, Consolacion, Naga), not just CBP.
  • Treat the valuation gap as a research signal; validate against transactions before operational use.
Future research
  • Put MCRAI on an empirical footing — estimate the decay rate, radii, and weights from data.
  • Test spatial heterogeneity directly with Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR / MGWR).
  • Enrich inputs — parcel attributes (frontage, corner-lot), a time dimension, macro indicators (Udomsap & Abid, 2020).
  • Triangulate against complementary reference prices, not a single target.

Closing
A defensible, reproducible template for local valuation analytics — a clearer starting point for deciding what a home is worth, not the last word.
TakeawayThe contribution is a demonstrated workflow — a custom MCRAI, a polycentric distance set, and a Random Forest + SHAP pipeline that can be rebuilt, checked, and adapted for other Philippine secondary cities.
