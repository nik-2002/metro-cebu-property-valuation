Two modeling traditions
Hedonic regression
  • Interpretable; one explicit coefficient per attribute.
  • The classic basis of property valuation (Rosen, 1974).
  • Struggles with non-linear effects and correlated features.
Machine learning
  • Captures non-linear, interacting effects (Breiman, 2001).
  • Tree ensembles tend to win on tabular data (Grinsztajn et al., 2022).
  • Regional precedent in Surabaya (Tanamal et al., 2023), with small-sample caution.

Location is the dominant driver
  • Four feature families recur: structural, economic / benchmark, geospatial / accessibility, amenity / point-of-interest.

Why model property types separately?

The decision to build separate models per property type is grounded in direct empirical evidence:

  • Dröes, Hoesli & Bourassa (2019) — modeling housing submarkets separately rather than pooling them lifted explanatory power substantially (R² from 0.637 to 0.782).
  • Usman, Lizam & Adekunle (2020), Malaysia — segmenting the market before fitting improved model fit by about 7% and cut prediction error by over 10%.
  • The intuition: different property types are priced by different logic, so one pooled equation averages those logics into a worse fit for all of them.

MCRAI — measuring access to amenities
The idea, in plain terms
  • For each property, count the nearby amenities in a category (schools, groceries, hospitals, …).
  • Closer amenities count more than far ones — the score fades with the square of road distance.
  • Each category has its own reach (radius), because a hospital serves a wider area than a corner store.

Distances in km, floored at 0.5 km. (Hansen, 1959)

MCRAI — how the weights were chosen
  • We did not assume the weights — we let the market reveal them, in two stages.
  • Stage 1: fit a regression and check which categories actually raise price (the sign).
  • Stage 2: for the positive ones, turn their strength into weights that sum to one.
  • Result: education 0.447, grocery 0.345, recreation 0.222.

Two more design choices
Neighborhood price (spatial lag)
  • The average price of nearby same-type listings within 500 m.
  • Captures Tobler's First Law — near things tend to be alike (Tobler, 1970).
Target variable
  • The model predicts the log of price per square meter.
  • Logging tames the skew; predictions are converted back for the price surface.

Evaluation protocol — an honest test

The headline numbers are estimated under a deliberately strict protocol.

  • GroupKFold(5), with groups defined by coordinate cluster.
  • The same location can never appear in both training and test folds.
  • This is stricter than a random 80/20 split — it stops the model from memorizing a neighborhood and flattering itself.
  • Every reported accuracy figure is out-of-fold.

A data-integrity story

One finding shaped how much the rest of the results can be trusted.

  • Early models were dominated by a single suspicious feature in the SHAP rankings.
  • The cause: the target had quietly come to mean two different things across scrape batches — the feature was acting as a scale-selector.
  • Tracing and fixing it (redefining the target to log price per square meter) is exactly why the final metrics are dependable.

A polycentric price surface

The SHAP results are the clearest evidence that the model learned a polycentric, not monocentric, structure.

  • CBP still anchors land and detached housing — the historic core matters most there.
  • But it does not stand alone: condominium weight spreads across the Mactan/airport corridor and the northern Mandaue–Consolacion nodes.
  • The reading is a corridor of linked centers, consistent with the JICA roadmap and polycentric theory (Giuliano & Small, 1991; McMillen, 2003; JICA, 2015).

Answers to the research questions
  • RQ1 — Location dominates; drivers differ by type (spatial lag for condos; CBP distance for houses and lots; amenities for bare land).
  • RQ2 — Tree models beat OLS everywhere; Random Forest best-or-tied with XGBoost, deployed for robustness and simplicity.
  • RQ3 — Geospatial features materially help, most where administrative benchmarks are weakest.
  • RQ4 — The BIR gap is large, positive, and systematic (~3× on the clean lot comparison).

Methodological

Limitations
  • Asking-price ceiling — trained on listings, not verified deed-of-sale transactions.
  • Cross-sectional snapshot — late 2025; no cyclical or temporal modeling.
  • Vacant-lot data ceiling — parcel attributes that drive land value are not recorded in listings.
  • Thin cells — some LGU×type combinations are too sparse for reliable single-cell estimates.

For practice and policy
Practice
  • Read the surface as a triangulation tool, alongside zonal values and comparables — not a final appraisal.
  • Weight polycentric distance and property type ahead of the amenity composite.
  • Treat the valuation gap as a research signal; validate against transactions before any operational use.
Policy
  • Keep recognizing secondary and corridor subcenters (Mactan, Consolacion, Naga), not just CBP.
  • Use MCRAI as an adaptable template, not a finished index.

For future research

Closing

This thesis should be read as a demonstrated, reproducible workflow more than a finished product.

  • A custom MCRAI design, a polycentric distance set, and a Random Forest + SHAP pipeline that can be rebuilt, checked, and adapted for other Philippine secondary cities.
  • What it finishes is a defensible template for local valuation analytics — a clearer starting point for the family deciding what a home is worth, not the last word.
