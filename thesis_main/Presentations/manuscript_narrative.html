Manuscript Narrative

Predicting Open-Market Residential Property Values in Metro Cebu · source notes for the defense deck · grounded in the manuscript, nothing invented
The spine — Metro Cebu has an active, fast-changing property market but no trustworthy, property-level price reference; this study builds one — open-market listings + engineered geospatial features + stratified, explainable ML — delivered as a web prototype, while measuring how far official benchmarks lag the market.

Ch 1 — Introduction

deck: The Property · The Market · The Question  •  manuscript pp. 1–9 (Research Questions p. 4)
  • Setup: Cebu is booming — 7.3% economic growth in 2024 (PSA Cebu, 2025), driven by IT-BPM and tourism; home prices rose 11.5% in 2025 (BSP, 2025), the highest rate outside Metro Manila.
  • The problem: a buyer or seller has no simple public source for what a property should command. Price is pieced from four partial references, each answering a different question: broker opinions (not standardized), bank appraisals (lending-risk basis), BIR zonal values (taxation, not live, often stale — BIR n.d.; Otsuka et al., 2023), online listings (asking prices, seller noise).
  • The diagnosis: the references aren't useless — they're fragmented (different purposes, assumptions, update speeds), so they don't compare. Metro Cebu lacks a transparent, spatially detailed reference layer.
  • Why now: prices outrun slow references; infrastructure (CBRT, Expressway) is redrawing accessibility; geospatial + listing data are now feasible.
  • The stance: this does not produce a final appraised value — it's a transparent decision-support layer that complements professionals (IVSC, 2025).
  • Research Questions: (1) value drivers; (2) best model among Hedonic / RF / XGBoost for deployment; (3) do geospatial features beat structural-only, and how by type; (4) how large is the gap vs BIR zonal.
  • Scope: 6 LGUs, 8 CBD nodes, open-market only deployed; web-app deliverable (a prototype for triangulation).

Ch 2 — Review of Related Literature

deck: The Field  •  manuscript pp. 10–19 (geospatial p. 14, spatial autocorrelation p. 16)
  • Traditional vs ML: hedonic (interpretable) vs ML; tree models tend to outperform on tabular data (Grinsztajn et al., 2022); regional evidence (Tanamal et al., 2023 — Surabaya RF), with a Tanzania RF (~53%) as a small-sample caution.
  • Philippine work is Manila / Pangasinan-centric (Viray, 2023; Ramolete et al., 2023) — little property-level, geospatially rich work.
  • Location is price: distance and accessibility are robust drivers; Agosto (2020) is the only Cebu-specific study — transport accessibility is the primary driver of Cebu land value. Accessibility indexing (Hansen, 1959); raw distances too fragmented alone (Rey-Blanco, 2024).
  • Macroeconomic factors acknowledged but out of scope (the study is cross-sectional).

Ch 3 — Methodology

deck: The Data (the "how it's built" slides)  •  manuscript pp. 20–37 (feature engineering p. 27, modeling strategy p. 31, metrics p. 34)
  • Design: quantitative, non-experimental; predictive + prescriptive.
  • Data: 3 listing portals, BIR zonal, geospatial APIs (Google / OSM); target = price per sqm; broker human-in-the-loop check.
  • Pipeline: ingest → clean → geocode → BIR → GIS features → ABT (funnel 16,561 → 3,616).
  • Network distance: shortest-path road distance to 8 polycentric nodes (osmnx Dijkstra) (Giuliano & Small, 1991; McMillen, 2003; JICA, 2015; Boeing, 2017).
  • MCRAI: gravity formula, β = 2 inverse-square decay; 8 amenity categories, each with its own radius (1–5 km); composite = the positive-OLS categories with weights edu 0.447 / grocery 0.345 / recreation 0.222 (Hansen, 1959).
  • Spatial lag: mean price of nearby same-type listings within 500 m (Tobler, 1970).
  • Modeling strategy: stratified (3 types) + per-stratum feature selection; OLS hedonic equation; leak-free GroupKFold; hyperparameter tuning.
  • Eval + explainability: MdAPE / PE20 headline, MAPE / COD / PRD supporting; SHAP. Deliverable: the web app.

Ch 4 — Data Understanding

deck: The Data (EDA)  •  manuscript pp. 38–47
  • Collection funnel by source (16,561 → 3,616; OnePropertee excluded for contamination).
  • Target: price per sqm is right-skewed; condos command the highest unit prices, vacant lots the lowest.
  • Geographic spread (uneven): Cebu City ~₱113,600/sqm, Mandaue ~₱96,100, Lapu-Lapu ~₱92,100; Talisay, Minglanilla, Consolacion form a lower band (~₱47,100–56,800).
  • Collinearity warning: several southern-corridor CBD distances move together — flagged for the linear baseline.
  • MCRAI zero rates: highest for security (26.6%), near-complete for education (0.3%) — coverage, not true absence.

Ch 5 — Data Preparation

deck: Groundwork  •  manuscript pp. 48–52
  • Cleaning, honestly: imputation kept visible (flags); structurally-absent fields (e.g., beds/baths for lots) left unimputed, not faked; hard duplicates dropped; a price-per-sqm sanity band applied.
  • Modeling subset: 3,372 of the 3,616 assembled ABT; target log-transformed; split into the three strata.
  • The data-integrity story: one scale-selector feature was quietly dominating the target (it had come to mean two different things across scrape batches); tracing and fixing it is why the metrics can be trusted.

Ch 6 — Modeling

deck: The Build  •  manuscript pp. 53–58 (per-stratum feature sets p. 53)
  • Why stratify: the condo median runs ≈ 5.8× the vacant-lot median — built area vs land-only are different price logics; pooling blurs them (Dröes et al., 2019; Usman et al., 2020).
  • Three strata: Condominium 1,300 · Houses 1,223 · Vacant Lot 849.
  • Per-stratum feature sets trimmed by VIF + OLS significance + ablation + MCRAI zero rates → 21 / 24 / 22 features. Condos & houses use the MCRAI composite; lots use 6 individual MCRAI categories.
  • The lineup: OLS hedonic baseline (HC3 robust) (Rosen, 1974), Random Forest (Breiman, 2001), XGBoost (Chen & Guestrin, 2016), SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017).
  • Evaluation: leak-free GroupKFold by coordinate cluster — the same spot can't be in train and test.

Ch 7 — Evaluation

deck: The Appraisal  •  manuscript pp. 59–66 (ablation p. 61)
  • Headline accuracy (MdAPE / PE20): Condo 19.3% / 51% · Houses 22.7% / 44% · Vacant Lot 38.4% / 26%. Supporting: MAPE, COD, PRD. Not claiming IAAO compliance.
  • Model comparison: tree models beat OLS in every stratum; RF edged XGBoost (19.3 vs 19.8 / 22.7 vs 23.6 / 38.4 vs 40.2) → deployed for robustness + simplicity.
  • Ablation (Structural / +Administrative / +Geospatial): Condo 24.9 → 23.0 → 19.3 · Houses 27.1 → 22.3 → 23.0 · Lot 51.5 → 42.3 → 38.6. Geospatial improves every stratum vs structural-only (+5.7 / +4.2 / +13.0).
  • Peer benchmark: comparable studies report MAPE ~48–53% (Nyanda, 2024) — context that the errors are competitive.

Ch 8 — Results & Discussion

deck: The Appraisal (SHAP + interpretation)  •  manuscript pp. 67–72
  • Three points: (1) location dominates every stratum; (2) drivers differ by type — condos → neighborhood price / spatial lag; houses & lots → distance to Cebu Business Park; lots → individual amenities; (3) MCRAI is selective, not uniform.
  • Polycentric, not monocentric: a corridor with several active peaks. CBP still anchors land and houses (bid-rent), while condos spread weight across the Mactan / airport / Consolacion nodes (Giuliano & Small, 1991; McMillen, 2003; JICA, 2015).
  • Mactan submarket: a bundle effect (airport access, bridge connectivity, tourism-adjacent condos), not a raw "island premium."
  • MCRAI + spatial sorting: negative signs on security / tourism / retail density reflect sorting and congestion, not that the amenity is harmful (Tiebout, 1956; Bayer & McMillan, 2012; Yang et al., 2016).

Ch 9 — Conclusions

deck: The Close  •  manuscript pp. 73–78 (RQ answers p. 73)
  • RQ1 — Location dominates; drivers differ by type (polycentric accessibility + submarket segmentation, not one citywide gradient).
  • RQ2 — Tree models beat OLS everywhere; Random Forest best-or-tied, deployed.
  • RQ3 — Geospatial features materially help, most where administrative benchmarks are weakest (condos, lots).
  • RQ4 — Vacant-lot market price ≈ 3× the BIR benchmark overall — from ~2.2× in Cebu City to ~4.8× in Mandaue; large, positive, systematic.
  • Contributions: methodological (per-type modeling, polycentric distances, two-stage MCRAI, leak-free CV); practical (a reproducible, explainable web prototype — TypeScript + Leaflet + Python).
  • Limitations: asking-price ceiling (not deed-of-sale), cross-sectional snapshot, vacant-lot data ceiling, thin peripheral cells.

Ch 10 — Recommendations

deck: The Close  •  manuscript pp. 79–end
  • Practice: read the surface as triangulation, not a final number; weight polycentric distance + property type over the amenity composite; treat the valuation gap as a research signal, not a correction factor (validate vs transactions first).
  • Policy: keep recognizing secondary / corridor subcenters (Mactan, Consolacion, Naga), not just CBP; MCRAI as an adaptable template, not a finished index.
  • Future research: estimate MCRAI parameters (β, radii, weights) from data; test GWR / MGWR for spatial heterogeneity; enrich parcel attributes (frontage, corner-lot) and add a time dimension + macro indicators (Udomsap & Abid, 2020).
  • Close: a demonstrated, reproducible workflow — a defensible template for local valuation analytics, not the last word.
