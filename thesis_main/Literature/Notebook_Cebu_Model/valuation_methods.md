| **Direct Comparison (Market Approach)**                       | Residential properties; Land (where comparable sales exist); Homogenous assets like vehicles or standard equipment [1], [2], [3], [4]. | **Price of Subject = Price of Comparable ± Adjustments.** Compares subject to similar assets sold recently, adjusting for differences in size, location, and condition based on the principle of substitution [5], [3], [6]. | Quickest and easiest method to calculate; widely trusted and understood by market participants [7], [8], [9].                     | Difficult to find identical comparables due to asset heterogeneity; adjustments are subjective; reliable data is often scarce in thin or informal markets [7], [9], [10], [11]. | Considered the "gold standard" globally, but highly speculative in developing economies (e.g., Nigeria, Kenya) due to data deficits and informal transactions [12], [13], [14], [15], [16]. | Automated Valuation Models (AVMs), Geographic Information Systems (GIS) [17], [18].              | [12], [1], [7], [8], [5], [9], [2], [13], [14], [17], [3], [15], [16], [18], [4], [6], [10], [11] |
| **Income Approach (Investment Method)**                       | Commercial real estate (offices, shopping centers); Income-generating assets [19], [20].                                               | **Value = Net Operating Income / Capitalization Rate.** Converts future income streams (rent) into a present capital value [21], [22].                                                                                       | Reflects investor behavior and the property's ability to generate specific financial returns [19], [20].                          | Sensitive to changes in capitalization rates; difficult to apply in markets with unreliable rental data or where owner-occupation dominates [23], [24].                         | widely used in formal markets; challenging in volatile African economies where inflation distorts rental values and capitalization rates are hard to derive [25], [26], [24].               | Financial Modeling Software (Excel, ARGUS), Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) tools [23], [20].         | [25], [21], [26], [23], [19], [22], [24], [20]                                                    |
| **Cost Approach (Depreciated Replacement Cost)**              | Public sector assets (schools, hospitals); Specialized properties; Insurance valuations [27], [28], [29].                              | **Value = (Cost of Land) + (Cost to Rebuild - Depreciation).** Estimates value based on current cost to replace the asset minus physical/functional/economic obsolescence [30], [28].                                        | Useful when no market data exists or for unique properties; provides a value for insurance purposes [31], [18].                   | Does not reflect market value (what a buyer pays); measuring depreciation is subjective; cost does not equal value [32], [30], [33].                                            | Often a method of last resort in developing countries (e.g., Tanzania, Sri Lanka) when market data is missing, but risks overvaluation [34], [35], [32], [28], [36].                        | Construction Cost Estimation Software, BIM (Building Information Modeling) [27], [29].           | [31], [30], [34], [35], [32], [27], [28], [33], [36], [29], [18]                                  |
| **Residual Method**                                           | Development sites; Land with redevelopment potential [37], [38].                                                                       | **Land Value = GDV - (Construction Costs + Fees + Developer's Profit).** GDV = Gross Development Value [39], [40].                                                                                                           | Explicitly values the land's potential; useful for feasibility studies [41], [37].                                                | Highly sensitive to small changes in inputs (GDV/Costs); highly hypothetical if development parameters are uncertain [42], [38].                                                | Common in urbanizing regions (e.g., UK, Malaysia) for valuing potential development sites; critical where land scarcity drives redevelopment [40], [43], [37].                              | Development Feasibility Software (ARGUS EstateMaster), Advanced Spreadsheet Modeling [39], [43]. | [41], [40], [42], [39], [43], [37], [38]                                                          |
| **Profit Method (Accounts Approach)**                         | Properties valued on trade potential (Hotels, Cinemas, Petrol Stations) [44], [45].                                                    | **Value = (Gross Profit - Operating Expenses - Tenant's Share) / Cap Rate.** [46].                                                                                                                                           | Values the operational business component tied to the real estate; useful where no comparables or rental evidence is absent [44]. | Difficult to separate the value of the physical property from the business goodwill/management skill; complex apportionment [45], [44].                                         | Used for specialized trading properties globally and in markets like Nigeria for hospitality assets [46], [45].                                                                             | Financial accounting software, Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems [46].                  | [44], [46], [45]                                                                                  |
| **Computer-Assisted Mass Appraisal (CAMA) / Hedonic Pricing** | Property taxation; Urban planning; Large portfolios [47], [48], [18].                                                                  | **Value = Function of Characteristics (Size, Location, Amenities).** Uses statistical regression to estimate values for large groups of properties [49], [18].                                                               | Efficient, fast, and consistent for large numbers of properties; reduces individual human bias in taxation [50], [51].            | Requires large, high-quality datasets to be accurate; "garbage in, garbage out" risk if data is poor [50], [18].                                                                | Employed in Rwanda and Turkey for tax mapping; difficult in third-world contexts lacking digital registries [52], [18], [51].                                                               | AVMs, Machine Learning (Random Forest, XGBoost), GIS [53], [42], [54].                           | [52], [49], [47], [50], [48], [53], [42], [18], [51], [54]                                        |
| **Proxy-Based / AI Valuation**                                | Informal markets; Data-poor environments; Disaster risk assessment [55], [56].                                                         | **Value Proxy = Satellite Imagery features (Roof Material, Nightlights).** Uses ML to correlate visual features with economic value [57], [56], [58].                                                                        | Provides objective value indicators where no formal transaction records exist; scalable across large regions [55], [56].          | Measures are "coarse" (e.g., nightlights miss informal economy); AI models may suffer from class imbalance in training data [59], [60].                                         | Emerging use in Sub-Saharan Africa (Kigali, Dar es Salaam) to bridge data gaps in the informal sector [60], [42].                                                                           | Remote Sensing, Computer Vision (CNNs, VLMs), Earth Observation (EO) data [55], [61].            | [57], [55], [59], [56], [60], [58], [61], [42]                                                    |
