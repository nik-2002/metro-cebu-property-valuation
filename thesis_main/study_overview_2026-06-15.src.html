A Data-Driven Residential Price Map for Metro Cebu

From online listings and geography to a tested machine-learning price surface — the full study, with formulas, plots, and plain-language explanations.

·Quick summary (30 seconds)

This study built a tool that estimates the open-market asking price per square metre of a residential property anywhere in Metro Cebu — from where it is, what's around it, and its basic characteristics — and delivers it as an interactive web app with a colour-coded price surface.

1The business problem

Metro Cebu is one of the fastest-growing property markets in the Philippines, yet there is no single, transparent, up-to-date reference for residential value. Brokers use personal know-how; banks appraise for collateral; the government's BIR zonal values update only every few years and are widely outdated; online listings are current but carry "asking-price" noise.

In plain terms — "BIR zonal value" is the Bureau of Internal Revenue's official price-per-square-metre for each small district (barangay), used mainly for taxes. It's an administrative floor, not a live market price, and is often years out of date.

The study fills the gap with a data-driven price surface trained on thousands of real listings, and flags where official values diverge from the market.

3What the research literature says

Decades of research support the idea that where a property is — and how easily it reaches jobs and amenities — is built into its price.

  • Hedonic pricing — price = sum of the values of a property's parts. Backbone of property modelling.
  • Bid-rent theory — buyers pay more for better access; value falls with distance. Strongest for vacant land.
  • Polycentric urbanism (Giuliano & Small, 1991) — metros have several centres; justifies eight nodes.
  • Hansen accessibility / gravity model (Hansen, 1959) — nearby amenities count more, fading with distance. The engine behind MCRAI.
  • Spatial autocorrelation — near properties have similar prices; justifies the neighbourhood-price feature.

Reference research also confirmed three design choices for the custom index: deriving weights from data (two-stage hedonic), a distance-decay setting β = 2.0 within the documented 1.75–2.0 range for congested developing-country cities, and category-specific reach radii rather than one fixed radius.

Why not an off-the-shelf equity map (e.g., OHANA)? Equity frameworks find where services should be (policy), not what the market pays. They use coarse 1 km grids, straight-line distances, and equal weights — wrong for parcel-level pricing. So a market-calibrated index was built instead.

Closest comparable studies

  • Ramolete et al. (2023), The Philippine Statistician — nearest Philippine study: Lamudi + amenities + government indicators + tree models for Cavite/Manila houses, ~10.7–21% error. Main benchmark.
  • Agosto (Cebu City) — only prior Cebu work; ranked mobility and livability as the top value drivers.
  • Tiglao & Rivera (2006, Manila) and SE-Asian work (Bangkok condos: transit ≈52% of value; Jakarta) — accessibility is central in congested Asian cities.

Citations drawn from the project's reference/literature notes; confirm exact details before formal academic use.

5Cleaning the data

Raw listings are messy. A transparent filter "funnel" reduced many raw rows to a trustworthy set:

  • Open-market only — bank foreclosures and government floor-price records removed (different value types).
  • Distressed "pasalo"/assume-balance listings removed (loan-takeover deals priced below market).
  • Sanity limits on price and size; de-duplication of relistings.
  • Spatial cap — at most ~3 listings per ~11 m map cell, so one over-listed building can't dominate.
A bug caught and fixed. Early data measured the target two ways across scrape batches (total price vs price-per-sqm), so the model partly "learned" the batch. The target was rebuilt consistently as price per square metre, making the models honest and interpretable.

7Feature engineering & the MCRAI formulas

"Features" are the input clues the model uses. Beyond area, bedrooms, and bathrooms, the study engineered location features — its core contribution.

  • Distance to the 8 economic centres along the real road network (shortest-path), so the Mactan bridge correctly adds distance.
  • MCRAI — the Metro Cebu Residential Accessibility Index (below).
  • BIR zonal value for the district; neighbourhood price level (same-type listings within 500 m); city.
In plain terms — MCRAI scores how well-served a location is by nearby amenities (schools, groceries, health, hospitals, recreation, etc.), using the "gravity" idea: closer amenities count more, and each amenity type has its own sensible reach distance.

The MCRAI formulas

Step 1 — per-category accessibility (Hansen gravity). For a property i and amenity category c, add up the "pull" of every amenity of that type within the category's reach radius Rc; nearer amenities count much more (the pull falls with the square of distance):

Per-category accessibility score
Ac(i)  =  Σ j ∈ c,  dij ≤ Rc   1  /  max( dij , 0.5 ) β
where dij = road-network distance (km) from property i to amenity j;   β = 2.0 (distance-decay);   the 0.5 km floor prevents very-near amenities from blowing up the score.

The reach radius differs by amenity type (everyday needs are walk-scale; hospitals and schools pull from farther):

CategoryRadius RcCategoryRadius Rc
Hospitals5.0 kmHealth (clinics)2.0 km
Tourism3.0 kmGrocery2.0 km
Education2.5 kmSecurity2.0 km
Recreation1.5 kmRetail density1.0 km

Step 2 — the composite index. The categories are combined into one master score using weights that come from the data, not opinion:

MCRAI composite (used for condos & houses)
MCRAIcomposite(i) = 0.447 · Aeducation(i)  +  0.345 · Agrocery(i)  +  0.222 · Arecreation(i)

How the composite weights were derived (two-stage hedonic)

The weights are not guessed — they are read off the market itself:

Stage 1 — learn each amenity's market value (one OLS regression)
ln(price/sqm)i = α + Σc βc · Ac(i) + (structural & control terms) + εi
Stage 2 — keep positive, significant amenities; normalise their values into weights
wc = βc  /  Σk ∈ positive βk

In plain terms: run one regression to learn how much buyers actually pay for each kind of nearby amenity (its "implicit price," βc); keep only the categories with a clear positive effect; and turn those into weights that add to 1. Here, education, grocery, and recreation came out positive and were kept. (Transport was retired in favour of direct road-distance features; and security, tourism, and retail density had negative effects — a "people sort away from congestion" signal — so they're kept only as standalone inputs, not in the composite.)

One smart twist: different feature sets per property type

  • Condos & houses use the single combined MCRAI score — for built homes, "is the area well-served overall?" is the right question, and the blend is simpler and slightly more accurate than the raw parts.
  • Vacant lots keep the six individual amenity scores — bare land responds to specific access (a market, a hospital) that the blend would average away.

9Modeling

Three model families were compared per group: hedonic regression (OLS) — the transparent straight-line baseline; Random Forest — the deployed model; and XGBoost — a strong comparison.

In plain terms — a "Random Forest" asks hundreds of slightly different "decision trees" (rules like "if large AND near the business park, lean higher"), each on a random slice of the data, then averages their answers. Averaging many imperfect opinions gives a stable, accurate estimate that handles messy data well.

Why the models can't just stack every feature: multicollinearity

Many location features move together (a well-connected area is near roads and amenities and pricey neighbours). A "VIF" check measures this overlap; high overlap was one reason redundant inputs were trimmed.

10★ The journey, tuning & how we know it works

10.1 — From a "good-looking but unusable" first model to today

The first attempt was a single model for everything (all types pooled, predicting total price, tested with a naive random split). It scored a high goodness-of-fit (R² ≈ 0.81) that looked great, but its typical percentage error was about 59% — unusable — and it carried the hidden target bug. 10.3 — Honest evaluation

Because the goal is a price map, the model must be judged on locations it has never seen.

In plain terms — "leakage" and the fix. If two listings on the same spot are split across training and testing, the model has "seen the answer" and looks better than it is. The fix — GroupKFold by location — keeps same-location listings together, so testing is always on unseen places. Tougher, but honest.

The scoreboard, with formulas

Error ratio & the metrics (computed on price/sqm, back-transformed)
ri = ŷi / yi  (predicted ÷ actual)

MdAPE = mediani | ŷi − yi | / yi × 100   (typical error — the headline)
MAPE = meani | ŷi − yi | / yi × 100   (average error — pulled up by a few extreme cases)
PE20 = ( count of | ŷi − yi | / yi ≤ 0.20 ) / n × 100   (share within ±20%)
COD = 100 × meani | ri − median(r) | / median(r)   (consistency — IAAO)
PRD = mean(r) / ( Σŷi / Σyi )   (fairness across cheap vs dear — ~1.0 is neutral)

COD/PRD come from the IAAO appraisal "ratio study" standards. The models don't meet the strict assessment-roll bands — expected for a listings-based prototype tested the hard way — and this is reported openly.

Diagnostic checks

10.4 — So, is it performing "okay"?

  • Versus the classic regression (OLS): Random Forest clearly wins on every group.
  • Versus the BIR benchmark: using official values alone is far worse — the model adds real information.
  • Versus the closest Philippine study (Ramolete et al., 2023): tested their easier way, the typical condo error is ~16% and houses ~22% — right at the top of their reported 10.7–21% band.

What drives each prediction (SHAP)

In plain terms — SHAP shows, for the model overall and for a single property, which features pushed the estimate up or down — turning a black box into something an appraiser can check. Each dot is one listing; position shows its push on the estimate.
Honest framing. These models are not assessment-grade and don't match mature-market systems — expected, given they learn from asking prices in a market with no public sale registry, a modest sample, and limited features. They are a credible first Cebu-specific decision-support prototype. Vacant lots stay weakest because bare-land value depends on parcel details absent from listings (frontage, zoning, title, slope, flood) — a data ceiling, not a broken model.

11Key insights

  • Location is king (RQ1) — geography carries most of the predictive power; size matters for built homes, almost everything for bare land.
  • Tree models tie; both beat regression (RQ2) — Random Forest chosen for robustness/simplicity.
  • Geography helps every group, differently (RQ3) — condos: overall accessibility; lots: specific access + distance to the main CBD. This justified the tailored feature sets.
  • Market sits well above official values (RQ4) — for vacant land, ~2–4× the BIR benchmark.
  • Honesty as a feature — the apparent accuracy "losses" along the way were the model becoming honest (earlier numbers were flattered by easy samples and leaky testing).
  • Documented limits — listings ≠ sales; vague-address geocoding noise; one portal ran cheaper; the vacant-lot data ceiling.

12Deployment

The three trained models are saved as files alongside a manifest — one record of each model's settings, exact feature list, and honest performance. The manifest is the project's "source of truth," so the app and any report always agree. A repeatable pipeline runs the whole chain (add listings → road distances → accessibility & neighbourhood features → keep in-scope cities → split into groups → train & record), and the study also produces map layers (QGIS) and a valuation-gap layer showing how far the market sits above BIR values, district by district.

13The Streamlit web app

The public deliverable is a web app (Streamlit turns analysis into interactive web pages) with three screens:

  • Market Map — browse real listings, filter by type, view amenities and price patterns.
  • Price Surface — a colour-coded map of predicted price/sqm for a chosen property type; far-from-any-listing areas are greyed and labelled "low confidence."
  • Property Predictor — drop a pin; the app fills in the city and an estimated BIR value, then returns a predicted price/sqm and total, plus a plain-language list of the price drivers (SHAP).

Verified (June 2026) to load the current models, match each property's features exactly to its model, and produce sensible predictions; the price surface was rebuilt to the latest models.

14Recommendations

How to use it

  • Treat outputs as a transparent reference for professional valuation/review — not a final figure (matches international valuation standards requiring human judgement).
  • Lean on the typical-error (MdAPE) figures and the per-property price drivers; treat greyed low-confidence areas with caution.
  • Use the valuation-gap layer to flag districts where BIR values lag the market.

How to improve it (future work)

  • Lift the vacant-lot ceiling with parcel attributes (zoning, flood/slope, frontage).
  • Re-geocode vague addresses to remove district-centre noise.
  • Add fine-grained neighbourhood statistics (e.g., barangay population density) where available.
  • Capture listing status at scrape time to filter distressed sales more reliably.

15Conclusion

Starting from messy online listings and an unusable first model, the study built a transparent, location-aware tool that estimates open-market residential prices across Metro Cebu, splits the market into condos, houses, and lots, and tests itself the hard, honest way. Typical errors — about 19% for condos, 23% for houses, and a candid 38% for vacant lots — make the typical condo and house estimate competitive with the closest published Philippine study, while being upfront about where it is weak and why. The contribution is the first Cebu-specific, geography-rich residential price surface — a custom accessibility index, eight polycentric centres, real road-network distances, and an honest evaluation protocol — delivered as a usable web app, positioned as decision support for appraisers, banks, buyers, and assessors.
